# Assessment: Mental Models and GenAI use & responsibility

**Type:** formative · **Formats:** quiz, reflection · **Grade share:** none · **ILOs assessed:** MM01, MM02, MM03, MM04, MM05, MM06, EPR08, EPR10, EPR11

## Items

### Q1 — MM01 — quiz (show of hands, start of session 2)
**Question** — When a GenAI chatbot answers, which is the best description of what it is doing?
(a) Searching the internet and copying the best match. (b) Looking the answer up in a database of facts. (c) Producing, one token at a time, a likely continuation of the conversation so far. (d) Reasoning about the question the way a person would.
**Answer** — (c). *(a)* probes the "it searches the web" misconception; *(b)* the "it's a database" model; *(d)* the "it's a mind" model.
**Where it sits** — session 2 opening.

### Q2 — MM03 — quiz (show of hands, start of session 2)
**Question** — A model gives you a confident, well-formatted reference to a paper that does not exist. Which limitation is this?
(a) Context-window overflow. (b) Hallucination. (c) Bias from the training data. (d) Non-determinism.
**Answer** — (b). *(a)* probes confusing "forgetting" with "inventing"; *(c)* confusing systematic skew with fabrication; *(d)* confusing variability with wrongness.
**Where it sits** — session 2 opening.

### Q3 — MM02 — quiz (show of hands, start of session 3)
**Question** — Put these in the order they happen when a model is made: alignment with human feedback (RLHF) · data collection · pre-training on next-token prediction.
**Answer** — data collection → pre-training → RLHF. Follow-up question to the room: *which of the three is where the knowledge-cutoff date comes from?* (data collection.)
**Where it sits** — session 3 opening.

### Q4 — MM04 — quiz (paper, end of session 3, 2 min)
**Question** — Name two tasks from today where GenAI added real value, and for each, one mistake it made or could have made that you had to check for.
**What a good answer shows** — two distinct use-case types (e.g. generating code, summarising) each paired with a plausible, specific failure (missing regex anchors; a summary line not in the source). Weak answers name tasks without failures.
**Where it sits** — session 3, after the use-case activity.

### R1 — MM05 — reflection (pre/post mindmap, session 1 + homework after session 4)
**Prompt** — Compare your two mindmaps. What did you believe that turned out to be a myth? What capabilities did you over- or under-estimate? What surprised you most?
**What a good answer shows** — at least one concrete belief revised with a reason grounded in the course (e.g. "I thought it looked things up; the simulation showed it samples from patterns"). Not: "I learned a lot".
**Where it sits** — session 1 (pre), homework after session 4 (post).

### R2 — MM06 — reflection (form W2, session 3)
**Prompt** — For each use case: was using GenAI appropriate here? Consider ethics (whose data, whose work), practicality (could you verify it?) and the task (does the value come from you doing it?).
**What a good answer shows** — a judgement that differs between the two cases, or a justified reason why it doesn't, referencing at least two of the three considerations.
**Where it sits** — session 3, use-case activity.

### R3 — EPR08 — reflection (homework sheet H1, reviewed in session 3)
**Prompt** — Pick one or two areas where you might be building up cognitive debt. What skill is it, what is the tool doing instead of you, and when would you notice?
**What a good answer shows** — a named skill (debugging, writing first drafts, reading documentation), a specific substitution, and a realistic moment of "paying interest" (exam, interview, incident).
**Where it sits** — homework before session 3; discussed in the LA12 group activity.

### R4 — EPR11 — reflection (homework after session 4)
**Prompt** — Write your own GenAI use plan for this degree, half a page: what you will use it for, what you will not, and how you will document its use. The University policy's AI use statement is the minimum.
**What a good answer shows** — explicit allowed/not-allowed lists tied to *why* (learning value, policy, verification); a documentation habit more concrete than "I'll disclose it" (e.g. keeping prompt logs for coursework). A plan that says "I'll use it for everything" or "I'll never use it" without reasons is weak.
**Where it sits** — homework after session 4.
